# Vedlegg del III: chapters 21-30 ("What We Did with the Inheritance") against Book Constitution v2.3

Uavhengig dellesning, 18. oktober 2026. Linjenummer viser til kapittelfilene (ett avsnitt per linje). Språk: engelsk, som levert. Merk: notefilen til kapittel 29 har 16 nummererte noter (hovedrapportens første telling på 17 inkluderte kapitteloverskriften).

Two framing facts that affect several chapters:
- Part III runs from chapter 21 to chapter 30; chapter 30 is the **closing chapter of Part III**.
- `notes/29` contains 16 numbered notes; the body text carries markers 1-16 (11 used twice at lines 51 and 53), all matched.

## Chapter 21: The Family Is Not a House

- **MINOR (I.14, concrete before abstract).** The chapter is almost entirely definitional prose; the only concrete object is the drawing at line 9. No rule breach, but a native editor will find it the most abstract chapter of the Part.
- **MINOR (H.11 / I.17.3).** Line 35: "Before I defend it as an order to which Norway should return, I need to ask when it was possible, whose work sustained it, and which people it leaves outside the frame." Read in isolation this says the author intends to defend a national return to the mid-century order. Chapter 22 immediately disowns this, but the sentence is the one a hostile reviewer would quote. Suggest "Before anyone defends it…" or "Before I could defend…".
- **Covenant (H.11): clean.** Lines 19 and 21 confine "covenant" to the obligations between family members under God; no covenant status is attributed to Norway.
- **H.15 / H.16: clean.** Line 9 reports only what the drawing shows ("Michael is shown beneath Valentin"); note 2 states it is undated, unsigned and not reproduced. Consistent with chapter 31 line 9.
- **Figures (I.11): clean.** No numeric claims; the SSB definitions are dated in note 1 (section dated 3 June 2021, consulted 6 September 2026).
- **Language: clean.** No em dashes, no American spellings.
- **I.17.3 hostile quote:** line 35, as above.

## Chapter 22: The Mid-Century Picture

- **MAJOR (internal consistency / clarity).** Line 19: "Arie Back describes Yeva opening a home restaurant after Pavel's railway employment ended." *Pavel* is never introduced in Part III. He is first identified nine chapters later (chapter 31 line 11: "They were brothers, the sons of Yeva and Pavel"). The reader of chapter 22 cannot tell whether Pavel is Yeva's husband, son or lodger. Fix: "after her husband Pavel's railway employment ended". This also matters because chapter 29 line 11 makes a point of the newspaper "adding Paul" to Valentin's name; a reader who later learns the father was Pavel will wonder why the book does not address the obvious overlap.
- **MAJOR (I.11.5 citation verification).** Note 2 URL for *Folketelling 1960, Hefte V, NOS XII 151* returns 404. (Hovedrapporten: erstatning funnet og bekreftet, https://www.ssb.no/a/histstat/nos/nos_xii_151.pdf.) The 1801 census page (note 1) and the Deneault/Sarkadi DOIs return 403 (bot-blocking; the DOIs resolve).
- **MINOR (redundancy).** Line 23 ("Here, domestic work helped earn the household's income. Yeva and Pavel's different tasks belonged to the same undertaking.") repeats lines 19-21 and reads as an orphaned fragment.
- **MINOR (H.16 / place and time).** Line 19 gives Yeva's restaurant neither a place nor a decade. Note 4 says the date is withheld deliberately; a one-clause locator ("in Russia" / "before the family left for…") would stop a reader assuming a Norwegian setting. If the source packet does not supply it, say so in the text.
- **H.16: otherwise clean.** The account is attributed to Arie Back, through a Norwegian translation, with the limit stated in the body (line 19) and the note.
- **H.8: clean.** Lines 27-31 refuse both nostalgia and contempt for the mid-century home; no reader group is treated as unfinished.
- **Language:** clean apart from the fragment at line 23.
- **I.17.3 hostile quote:** line 19, because of the unexplained "Pavel".

- **Conway et al. (L3c, H.9a): compliant.** Line 19-21 carries country list, N, Catholic-parent sample, cross-sectional/retrospective status and the explicit non-transfer to Norway in the same passage. Verb is "had stronger positive associations". Clean.
- **Sarkadi (H.9): compliant.** "support for active, regular interaction", with country split, SES controls and confounding stated.
- **MINOR (I.14, British editor).** Line 13: "Children were aged three to 180 months." Mixed word/numeral and an unnatural range; write "from three months to fifteen years".
- **MINOR (I.14).** Line 7: "about 22,300 individual datasets": if that is Sarkadi's own phrase, fine; if not, "individual data sets" or "children" reads better. Needs source packet.
- **MINOR (notes).** Note 3 records a 9,319/9,313 discrepancy as unresolved; acceptable, but a publisher's check should settle it.
- **H.8: clean.** Line 29 ("respect necessary distance … I cannot praise paternal constancy in terms that make the child's protection seem like disloyalty") explicitly denies that staying is always right.
- **H.15: clean.** Line 29 is generic; no case, no antagonist.
- **I.17.3 hostile quote:** line 19, "recalled paternal church attendance had stronger positive associations than maternal attendance": the one father-over-mother sentence in the Part; it is fenced correctly, but it is the sentence a reviewer will lift without the fence.

## Chapter 24: An Intact Home That Was Not Safe

- **MAJOR (H.8 / I.17.3).** The book's single mandated "honest, extended account of a formally intact home that was not safe" is John Stuart Mill, and the chapter's own witness says, line 23: "His father's severity, he wrote, had not prevented a happy childhood." The "not safe" element is, by the chapter's own definition (line 31), "the difficulty of speaking freely to the person upon whom one depends": fear, not violence, neglect or abuse. A hostile reviewer will quote the title against line 23. The author ruling in the handoff ("Ja, bruk Mill med begge sider") stands, so this is not a BLOCKER, but the chapter should say in one sentence that it has deliberately chosen the mildest case (fear without violence) and that the harder cases follow in chapter 26; line 47 gestures at this but does not say it.
- **MINOR (I.14).** Line 27: "in terms many readers now will reject" should be "in terms that many readers will now reject".
- **MINOR (I.14).** Line 3: "he had been told that he was three" is fine, but the chapter later (line 37) introduces Hilmar without reminding the Anglophone reader who he is; a two-word gloss ("my grandfather Hilmar") would help a reader who starts at Part III.
- **Overclaim (H.2/H.9): clean.** Everything is tied to Mill's own account; note 1 flags the retrospective status and the told-not-remembered age; no diagnosis or prevalence inference.
- **H.8 second clause: clean.** The chapter never implies staying is always right; line 39 protects both the afraid child and the grateful one.
- **H.16: clean.** The Mills are dead but every detail is from the *Autobiography*; no invented weather, dialogue or motive. Line 7 ("Mill remembered an impatient man") attributes the characterisation.
- **Language:** no em dashes; British spelling throughout.
- **I.17.3 hostile quote:** line 23.

## Chapter 25: The People Norway Made Smaller

A reviewer will quote the title. Consider a title drawn from the chapter's own vocabulary ("Transition Districts", "Help with Conditions").
- **MINOR (I.14).** Line 13: "the convenient phrase minorities" needs italics or quotation marks around *minorities*.
- **MINOR (terminology).** Line 29: "to speak as a Sámi, Kven or Romani witness". Note 2 is careful not to conflate taterne/romanifolket with Roma; "Romani" in the body undoes that care for an Anglophone reader. Use the same compound as line 15 ("taterne/romanifolket", or "Tater/Romani people").
- **Overclaim: clean.** All claims are attributed to Innst. 30 S (2024-2025) and NOU 2015:7; dates (1851, 1898, 1921, 1936, 1897, 1907, 1986) are given; no figures.
- **H.7: partial.** Church failure, cultural messages and the author's own preference (line 23) are present; mothers, custody, economics, housing and working hours do not arise because no paternal failure is named here. Acceptable for this chapter.
- **H.11: clean.** Line 23 "To describe the nation as an inheritance" does not assign covenant status.
- **Register (D.1): clean.** Historical [H] with the author's inference marked ("I cannot invoke…").
- **I.17.3 hostile quote:** the title.

## Chapter 26: Behind the Closed Door

As written this can be read as "Norwegian mothers and [Norwegian] fathers who were soldiers…". Rewrite: "to Norwegian mothers and to fathers who were soldiers of the German occupying power".
- **MINOR (I.14).** Line 13: "An enduring arrangement must remain answerable for what it endures in doing." A native editor will flag "what it endures in doing" as unidiomatic.

What joins them for this argument is the inadequacy of judging an arrangement only by the fact that it has survived." Verbs are "identified", "found", "recorded".

- **I.17.3 hostile quote:** line 33.

## Chapter 29: The Machine That Cast Us

This chapter is 1,992 words (the others run 998-1,252) and consists of one paragraph of the author's own RO/lion material (line 3) followed by roughly 1,850 words of archival reconstruction of the 1938-39 Valentin Back informer allegation (lines 5-87), with a one-sentence turn to Norway at line 89.

- **BLOCKER (handoff status / H.3 / L.4).** The handoff (lines 6 and 25) says chapter 29's next task is "kapittel 29s konkrete selvaudit" of the RO pilot, that two questions on the pilot's self-presentation were "sendt, ikke besvart", that the Back text (2,469 words) sits "utenfor kapitteltotalen" with placement unresolved, and that "Noteporten for 0023I består". The chapter on file is the Back text placed as chapter 29, and the self-audit is one paragraph. Either the handoff is stale or this placement has not been authorised and the note gate has not been cleared. Needs confirmation before anything else is done to this chapter.
- **BLOCKER (H.3, L.4).** The self-audit paragraph (line 3) does not function as "evidence against his own temptation". Quoted in full: "I have used the image of a lion when writing about myself. Beside the ruined church, it gave me a way to describe standing up again, calling to God and refusing to go home defeated. Put more plainly, I was a father who had prayed for his son and found strength in doing so. My faith was in God. The coat, the borrowed drum and the image were ways of expressing it." That is a defence of the image, not an audit of it. L.4 requires "the author's admission that he used myth to organise chaos by assigning roles, together with the artefact he made and the contradictions in how he labelled it"; none of those three elements appears. Chapter 30 then uses the public scene to illuminate "a country doing something together" (line 31) before any such admission exists.
- **MAJOR (H.3; body/notes inconsistency).** Note 1: "the lion and **king** images are his own figurative language". The body never mentions a king image. A "king" figure applied to the author is exactly the mythic office H.3 forbids, and the note discloses what the body withholds. Either print the admission in the body or remove "king" from the note; the current split is the worst of both.
- **MAJOR (handoff/notes inconsistency).** Note 1 claims "Author's direct confirmation, 6 September 2026" for the lion/king wording; the handoff (same date) says the questions to the author on the RO pilot's self-presentation were unanswered. Needs confirmation of which is true.
- **MAJOR (H.16 "love is named as an unreliable witness"; I.17.2).** The chapter weighs the purpose of every source ("Each had a purpose that must be considered when weighing it", line 27) but never names the author's own purpose: Valentin Back is his great-grandfather and the chapter is, in effect, an attempt to lift an informer label from his family. Line 5 says only "In my family, one of those descriptions attached itself to Valentin Back, Michael's father." The author's stake must be declared in the body before the record is weighed.
- **MAJOR (H.2; claim stated as fact that the note labels unverified).** Line 41: "On 3 October 1938, according to the appeal draft written that day, a military court sentenced Yaakov Kotick to death" and line 67: "The sentence was passed on 3 October 1938". Note 9 says the sentence is "reported in the defence's own document" and "not verified against a contemporary official decision"; nothing in the notes says the sentence was passed on 3 October rather than reported in a draft dated 3 October. The "immediately" argument at lines 67-69 rests on this date. Write "on or before 3 October" or source the sentencing date. Needs source packet.
- **MAJOR (H.2; unsourced breadth).** Line 45: "Contemporary newspapers in several countries described Valentin Back as an informer, a provocateur, a prosecution witness." Only the Québec item is read; note 10 says Davar is "located but unread" and names no other paper. "Several countries", "provocateur" and "prosecution witness" are unsupported by the notes as they stand. Needs source packet listing the papers and the exact terms.
- **MAJOR (H.2).** Line 61: "Those findings correct the account of this operation." Both documents are the defence's interested submissions (as lines 31 and 57 themselves say). "Correct" is a verdict; "contradict" or "complicate" is what the evidence supports.
- **MINOR (H.2; needs source packet).** Line 23: "Between 18 and 26 September, Valentin Back was entered and summoned as a witness." Notes 4-5 support 20 and 26 September; no note supports 18.
- **MINOR (H.2; needs source packet).** Line 51: "By 23 June 1939, Valentin Back had been found **shot** dead." Note 11 says JTA "reports a body found early that day"; the manner of death is not in the note.
- **MINOR (H.16).** Line 73: "Yaakov Kotick sat down with an interviewer": a small invented sensory detail about a man who is presumably dead; "gave an interview to" is what the transcript establishes.
- **MINOR (I.15 / I.17.1 / I.17.8).** Lines 77-87 are an inventory of unread catalogue entries, ending (line 87): "The returns of capital sentences passed in Palestine in 1938 and 1939 are held at The National Archives at Kew, under reference CO 733/402/14. The catalogue card has been read. The file has not." This belongs in a note (note 16 already carries it). In the body it is the sentence a hostile reviewer quotes to show the chapter is unfinished archival homework, and it pulls the chapter toward autobiography/exceptional-identity territory (I.15).
- **MINOR (title; I.13.3).** "The Machine That Cast Us" promises the author's own role-casting machine (the self-audit) and delivers a press-and-court record about someone else. "Us" is unexplained.
- **MINOR (I.14, Anglophone reader).** Line 9: "testified against Revisionists" has no gloss; line 31 "Michael Elagine" shares a first name with the author's grandfather Michael and will confuse a reader of chapter 21.

- **MINOR (I.14).** Em dashes in the notes to chapter 29 (note 4, note 7).

## Chapter 30: The Roar on the Grass

- **BLOCKER (Constitution revision record; C.1).** "The public 2026 RO phenomenon … may not open, close or title a part." Chapter 30 is wholly the public 2026 scene and is the **last chapter of Part III**. As placed, the public RO material closes a part. Either move the chapter inside the Part, or end Part III with another chapter.
- **MAJOR (I.17.5; register).** Christ does not appear in the chapter at all, and the chapter's hope/joy (lines 43-45: "The distinction allows the scene to keep its joy… The question for me is what I do after looking at it") stands entirely without him. As the closing movement of a Part, that fails the Christ-removability test by construction.
- **MINOR (H.3 / I.13.3; title).** "The Roar on the Grass": the chant is "Ro", not a roar; "roar" reads as a continuation of the lion image the author applied to himself at chapter 29 line 3. If intended, a symbol H.3 bars from describing the author now titles the chapter; if unintended, it is a pun the reader will still make. Retitle (e.g. "Ro").
- **Public RO material (I.13): appears once and is delayed, confirmed.** Across all 41 chapter files the Haaland/Brazil/Viking Row material appears only in chapter 30 (the author's note mentions RO only as the H.13 conflict-of-interest disclosure). Length 1,162 words, within the C.1 4,000-word and C.2 1,500-word caps as far as Part III is concerned.
- **MINOR (C.2).** Line 3: "after Norway beat Brazil 2–1 on 5 July 2026": a result and fixture. It does date the photograph, which is arguably "necessary narrative work", but it is the only result in the chapter and could be moved to the note.
- **Norse material (I.13): passes narrowly.** Line 25 ("The temptation becomes stronger when the movement is given an old name. 'Viking Row' can make a recent supporter practice sound as though it had been waiting in the body for centuries… It does not establish the ancestry of the people performing it.") is framed as the author's temptation and rejected. Keep it exactly this short.
- **E.7 handling: compliant.** Lines 9, 23 and 33 say the author cannot know whether the meaning was in the crowd or in himself.
- **I.15 living persons: clean.** Haaland is described ("Haaland is standing… had led the celebration at the drum"), not cast as hero; Ole Frøystad is credited, not criticised; Brazil is a result, not an enemy. One gloss needed: line 7 does not say who Frøystad is (a supporter?).
- **Photograph.** Sourced: note 1 (AFP via Malay Mail, 6 July 2026; "The image is described, not reproduced"); match result checked against FIFA schedule per note (and independently confirmed in the main report). No copyright or licensing claim is made and none is needed for a verbal description. The front matter states no third-party photographs are reproduced. **MINOR:** the body never says so; "The AFP photograph was taken…" (line 3), "No family tree is visible in that photograph" (line 27) and "The photograph holds the men in place" (line 45) will send some readers looking for a plate. Add a clause in the body ("a photograph I describe but do not reproduce"). Whether the photograph was taken on 5 July (not 6 July) needs source packet.
- **MINOR (notes).** Note 3: "The author's earlier RO account places his experience of the public celebration on a screen." "Earlier RO account" could be read as an earlier chapter (which would breach "appears once"); name the document as the prologue notes do.
- **H.10 removability:** delete the scene and the Part's argument still stands; the chapter is illumination, as required. But see I.17.5 above.
- **Language: clean** (no em dashes; "2–1" is an en dash).
- **I.17.3 hostile quote:** line 23: "I cannot count the arms and multiply my childhood by their number." Quoted alone it reads as exactly what the book is doing with Hilmar and the crowd.

## Summary

Counts: **BLOCKER 3** (29: handoff/placement and note gate; 29: self-audit absent/defensive, H.3-L.4; 30: public RO scene closes Part III). **MAJOR 13** (22: Pavel; 22: dead 1960-census link; 24: Mill as the H.8 unsafe home; 26: krigsbarn sentence; 27: "before and during"; 28: title; 29: king image in note; 29: note-vs-handoff author confirmation; 29: author's own stake undeclared; 29: sentencing date; 29: "several countries"; 29: "correct the account"; 30: no Christ in the Part's closing chapter). **MINOR approx. 30** as itemised.

Three most important issues:
1. **Chapter 29 is not the chapter the Constitution and handoff require.** It is the Back case text placed under a self-audit title, with a one-paragraph defence of the lion/coat/drum imagery in place of the admission L.4 and H.3 demand; the notes reveal a "king" image the body omits, and the handoff says the author's answers on the pilot's self-presentation were still outstanding.
2. **Chapter 30 closes Part III with the public 2026 RO scene**, which the Constitution's revision record and C.1 forbid, and it is the only chapter in the Part with no Christological content at all.
3. **The H.8 "intact home that was not safe" is carried by Mill alone**, whose own verdict ("had not prevented a happy childhood", 24:23) is the sentence any reviewer will quote against the chapter title; the chapter needs one sentence owning that choice and pointing to chapter 26 for violence and economic entrapment.

Items marked "needs source packet": 22:19 place/time of the restaurant; 23:7 Sarkadi phrasing; 27:3 "before the occupation"; 29:23, 29:41/67, 29:45, 29:51; 30:3 photo date.
