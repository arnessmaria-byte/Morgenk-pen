# Vedlegg del IV: chapters 31-40 and Epilogue ("The Turning") against Book Constitution v2.3

Uavhengig dellesning, 18. oktober 2026. Linjenummer viser til kapittelfilene (ett avsnitt per linje). Språk: engelsk, som levert. Hovedrapporten har siden bekreftet de fire ordrette bibelsitatene mot EHV og EHV-notisens årstall 2017; de «needs source packet»-merkene som gjelder dette, er dermed lukket.

Cross-cutting checks that are clean for the whole part: no em dashes or en dashes in any body text; no 2 Timothy 3, Galatians 5, Ahab or Jezebel material anywhere in 31-41 (so the F.2/F.3/F.6 order-of-application rules are not engaged here); no date, timetable or predicted event (L10/H.5); no covenant status or "Christian nation" claim for Norway (H.11); no biblical enemy role assigned to a living person (I.10); no numbered steps, action lists or workbook apparatus (I.15); no youth slang; no recruiting language or pointer to the author's channel (H.13).

## Chapter 31. Who Gets to Say We

- **Scripture handling.** No Scripture quoted. Clean.
- **MINOR (production, I.11.3 / note apparatus).** Note markers are printed as run-together digits that read as "note 13" and "note 12": line 11 "…belonged to Michael's history too.13", line 21 "…in the account of Yeva.13". There are only three endnotes. These must be typeset as "1, 3" (superscript, with separator) or the reader will look for a thirteenth note. Same fault in chapter 32 (lines 17, 23, 27).
- **MINOR (H.16, needs source packet).** Sensory detail is attributed to Arie's own account (coffee, "heavy Russian dishes", "a thick tablecloth of artificial velvet", "ordering it out in Russian"), which is permitted as reported memory, and line 7 carries the witness's own warning ("He knew that his quarrels with their grandmother might colour what he wrote"). Cannot confirm from here that every detail is in the checked Norwegian translation; "Pavel's Hebrew name was Naftali; the official Russian name Arie found was Pantoleimon" (line 13) and "after his railway employment ended" (line 21) need the source packet (the usual transliteration is Panteleimon; if the document spells it Pantoleimon, keep it and say so in the note).
- **H.4 "we".** Used only where the author is genuinely included: line 15 "It gives us this grandson's explanation of these names"; line 33 "I want to be able to say we without making the people inside the word interchangeable." Clean. The chapter never uses "we" for Norwegians as a whole.
- **Relatives' beliefs.** No belief or conversion attributed to relatives; line 37 "It gives me no warrant to rewrite Jewish relatives as Christians they did not say they were" is consistent with the Author's Note. Clean.
- **MINOR (I.14, native-editor flags).** Line 3 "Feeding the children brought her into conflict with the boy who would later write about her" (clumsy periphrasis for Arie); line 19 "This is part of what I mean by recovering the Solbak Årnes family's Jewish history" (announces a definition the chapter never gives); line 29 "They cannot become memories of my Norwegian childhood through a change of pronoun" (good idea, awkward phrasing); line 35 "The difficulty grows when the word is used for a country" (the "difficulty" has not been named).
- **I.17.3 hostile sentence.** Line 35: "I cannot turn the Back papers into a general rule about who may call Norway home." A reviewer will ask why a chapter about a Russian-Jewish kitchen remembered by a cousin sits in a book whose title names Norway, and will quote the author conceding that it proves nothing about Norway.

- **MAJOR (internal consistency, I.11.5).** Two spellings of the same family name in one sentence pair: line 11 "It identifies her brothers, including Meir and Vladimir Grosman. The family drawing places Hava, or Yeva, beneath Eliah and Rachel Grossman." Chapter 40 line 17 then has "The Back and Grosman records". Chapters 21, 31, 33 and all notes use "Grossman". Either standardise or state once that the documents spell it both ways.
- **MINOR (note apparatus).** Run-together markers "12" (line 17, line 27) and "13" (line 23); see chapter 31.
- **MINOR (source provenance, I.12).** Note 2 sources "Michael's journeys and the author's memories" to "RO Voice Pilot 02, v0.5.1". Chapter 4 note 2 does the same. A memoir fact whose controlling source is an RO pilot script should be re-sourced to the author interview record, or the note should say the pilot merely preserves an author-confirmed memory, as note 6 of chapter 6 does.
- **Relatives' beliefs, approval instance, invented detail, Author's Note.** No belief or conversion is attributed to any relative. Line 31: "My relatives do not acquire that confession through their place on the tree." Line 23 "Gerda asked someone who knew the family to confirm their Jewish belonging" is consistent with chapter 4 (letter of 23 May 1947). No Christian approval instance over Jewish identity. No invented detail about the dead. Consistent with the Author's Note. Clean.
- **MINOR (I.17.3 / G.4).** Line 33 "I am Jewish and Norwegian." This is the author's own identity claim and he is entitled to it, but it is the sentence a hostile reviewer will set beside the front-matter Christian confession and ask who adjudicated the first word. The surrounding paragraph ("I am not asking a sequence of admired ancestors to guarantee my character") partly answers this; one sentence saying by what reckoning he uses the word (descent through Michael's line, as the family's own records state it) would close the gap.
- **I.14 flags.** Line 3-5 is the best writing in the part ("Hilmar could take me fishing because I was his grandson. Being his grandson could not teach me to fish."). Line 19 "Helping a teenager leave the country can increase the distance between him and the family that makes it possible" needs "the family that makes it possible" clarified. Line 27 "The garment he made commemorated Hilmar's working life" is unexplained for a reader who has not retained chapters 1-3; name the garment.
- **Hostile sentence.** Line 33: "I am Jewish and Norwegian."

## Chapter 40. The Man Who Stays

- **Scripture handling.** John 21:1-19 paraphrased (note 1, EHV, "no additional sensory detail or reconstructed dialogue"); line 7 verbatim "You follow me." (John 21:22, note 2, EHV, confirmed); 1 Corinthians 15:58 paraphrased (note 3, EHV). Clean.
- **H.3 / G.4.** Line 9 "it gives me no right to place myself in his apostolic office." Clean.
- **I.16 ending: closing paragraphs quoted.** Line 37: "At the end of his argument about resurrection, Paul tells the Corinthians that their labour is not in vain in the Lord. The words follow Christ's victory over death… The hope is large enough to include work whose worth has not been recognised, because its ground is the risen Christ rather than the audience that observes it." Line 39: "I cannot derive that hope from Norway's statistics, from my ancestry or from a favourable account of my own conduct. Those things cannot establish it. I confess it as Christian hope, and I remain answerable for the claims I have made in its name." Line 41: "It leaves the country loved without being made sacred. It leaves the family precious without making its restoration a condition of salvation. It leaves a younger man free to receive a good inheritance, question a false account and take up a responsibility which belongs to him. No public role is required before that work can matter." Line 43: "The food on the shore is given before Peter can accomplish the work entrusted to him. He is called by someone who has already risen. His answer will cost him, but he does not have to supply the life from which it begins."
  Judgement: hope is classified as hope and expressly refused a statistic (I.16 met); the last movement belongs to Christ and arrives as gift (met); the cost is named ("His answer will cost him"); the case where the other heart does not turn is carried by 39 and echoed at line 35. What is thin is the *concrete* in L12 ("the phone call, confession, restored Sunday, truthful boundary, or man who stays"): the chapter speaks of "an hour rather than a generation" (line 25) and "a modest duty" (line 27) but never shows one. The Epilogue is evidently meant to supply the concrete scene; see below.
- **MINOR (consistency).** Line 17 "The Back and Grosman records" (see chapter 32 spelling).
- **I.14 flags.** Line 25 "This leaves a man with work that can look small beside the problems considered in a book about a country." Line 11-13 "One test comes… Another comes…" edges toward the men's-group register (I.15) but stays in prose.
- **Hostile sentence.** Line 25: "Keeping that promise does not prove a theory of national renewal." A reviewer will say the book has spent forty chapters on a theory it now declines to defend.

## Epilogue. The Next Room

- **Scripture handling.** None. Clean.
- **BLOCKER (I.16 / H.1 / I.9, the last movement).** The book's final words are: line 11 "At home, I have my own part to do. On Sunday, usually with just Maria beside me, I lead the little service in our living room. She has baked the bread. We break it together." Communion is Christ's gift, but Christ is not named anywhere in the epilogue; the only religious nouns are "a short service", "the Jesus Prayer", "Communion", "the Bible and unleavened bread". The grammatical subject of the last two sentences is the couple, and the verb of the last paragraph is "I lead". Read against I.16 ("the last movement belongs to Christ and arrives as gift, not as the author's achievement") and H.1 ("hope must be locatable in Christ's resurrection rather than in the home"), the epilogue relocates the hope that chapter 40 placed on the shore into the author's living room. One sentence making the giver explicit (the bread broken is given, not made; the risen Christ of chapter 40 is the host) would satisfy I.16 without changing the scene. Without it, the ending fails I.17.5 for the epilogue taken alone.
- **MAJOR (I.16 "includes the case where the other party does not turn"; H.15; I.17.6).** The prologue opens with the author praying for his son beside a ruined church ("My son had not come home with me", prologue line 17). The epilogue shows a complete household (wife, daughter, dog) and does not mention the son at all. H.15 rightly forbids the case, but it does not forbid the absence; the approved wording is about absence. As printed, the ending of a book about a father who cannot reach his son is a Sunday with the daughter and the dog, and the thirty-year test cuts both ways: the son, reading at thirty, will see himself omitted from the last page. One clause of the approved kind ("the one who is not in the room", or the prologue's own "the ordinary freedom I wanted had not been restored") belongs here, placed before the bread, so that the costly case stands inside the ending rather than three chapters before it.
- **MAJOR (I.12 living private persons; first appearance).** Maria, Sunniva and the dog Elisja appear here for the first time in the book (no earlier chapter names them). Naming a living minor child in the final page needs the same consent and thirty-year test as the son; nothing printed about her is exposing, but the note should record author consent for the daughter's name as it does for the Hilmar/Michael material. The decision to name the daughter and not the son should be a conscious one, stated in the production record.
- **MINOR (note apparatus).** The epilogue text carries no note markers, but the notes list entries 1 and 2. Add markers or convert the notes to an unnumbered source note.
- **MINOR (I.14, sentences a native editor would flag).** Line 5 "She had searched for the Bible and unleavened bread and found articles describing manna bread" (garbled; presumably "searched for what the Bible says about unleavened bread"). Line 3 and line 11 repeat the same information ("I lead a short service… share Communion" / "I lead the little service… She has baked the bread") in 223 words. Line 7 "These are parts of our ordinary life, repeated on different days" (filler). Line 9 "accounts whose tellers I can name" (awkward).
- **MINOR (I.17.3).** A hostile reviewer on the Communion scene: a layman consecrating at home with "a version inspired by those recipes" and "wine beside it" will draw comment from confessional readers of both Lutheran and Catholic kinds. The constitution does not forbid it, but the author should know the sentence is quotable.
- **Hostile sentence.** Line 11: "On Sunday, usually with just Maria beside me, I lead the little service in our living room." A reviewer: a book about inheritance across generations ends with a congregation of two.

## Summary

**Counts.** BLOCKER 1 (Epilogue last movement). MAJOR 6 (Grosman/Grossman spelling; ch 37 "when God will punish a country"; Epilogue omits the unturned heart; Epilogue first-appearance/consent of named family; plus the two part-wide items below). MINOR about 30, mostly line-edit and apparatus.

**Part-wide (Scripture translation record, I.11.5, front matter).** The front matter states EHV. Every note in 33-40 attributes to EHV with a biblegateway EHV link (all return 200). The handoff says of chapters 35-38: "WEB British er fortsatt arbeidsoversettelse" and that only the main agent checked the primary texts. The four verbatim quotations (Matt 10:37, Mal 4:5-6, Rom 12:18, John 21:22) have now been confirmed as EHV in the main report, so the text was conformed to EHV; one WEB trace survives in paraphrase (ch 39 line 21). The handoff wording should be updated, and "LORD" small capitals in Malachi checked against printed EHV.

**Part-wide MAJOR (I.14 "concrete before abstract; scene before thesis").** Chapters 33-39 contain no scene other than the Scripture scenes. Modal density: "cannot" appears 14, 10, 11, 6, 5, 16 and 14 times in chapters 33-39 respectively (about one per 80 words), with "must" and "should" on top. The prose is written almost entirely as prohibitions and disclaimers, and the same disclaimer that the author's confession does not supply his relatives' beliefs is repeated in 31 (line 37), 32 (line 31), 33 (line 11), 34 (line 35), 35 (line 25), 37 (line 19) and 40 (line 21). Said once, it is integrity; said seven times, it reads as legal insurance and a hostile reviewer will count them. Recommend keeping it in 31 and 33 and cutting the rest.

I want relationships to endure… Yet I cannot use the same question to decide whether Christ deserves a place." and line 29: "There is a loss for my preferred argument in admitting this. 4. Ch 39 line 29-31: "His death and resurrection are not guarantees that my family will acquire the ending I can imagine… I do not find this an easy consolation. It removes the bargain by which I might offer enough obedience to purchase a result."
Also ch 34 line 41 ("It cannot redeem Norway, and it cannot redeem me") and ch 40 line 39. The threshold of three is met. Weakness: all four correct "the book I wanted to write" rather than a position the reader has actually seen the author hold in print; the costliest one (39) is tied to the son and is therefore real. Recommend that at least one correction be anchored to a specific earlier printed position (e.g. the inheritance inference of L6, or the chapter 29 self-audit), so a reviewer cannot say the author is correcting a straw version of himself.

**Three most important issues in this part.**
1. The Epilogue's last movement belongs to the author's living room, with Christ unnamed and the son unmentioned (BLOCKER plus two MAJOR). Two sentences fix it: name the giver of the bread, and let the empty chair stand in the room in the approved wording.
2. The translation record was internally inconsistent (front matter and notes say EHV; the handoff says chapters 35-38 were drafted on WEB British). The four verbatim quotations are now confirmed EHV; the handoff should be corrected and the one WEB paraphrase aligned.
3. Chapter 37 line 29 "Social statistics cannot tell me when God will punish a country" is the single quotable sentence that undoes the chapter's otherwise careful refusal to aim Malachi's curse at Norway; change "when" to "whether".
