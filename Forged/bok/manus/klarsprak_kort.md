# Plain-English style card: FORGED (round 5)

## The author's request

The author wants the whole book in plain English: "as down-to-earth, simple and easy to understand as at all possible." The author named the problem directly. Sentences like "Witness is not performance" sound wise but do not do the job. A young man reading at 7 a.m. has to understand every sentence on the first read, and be able to explain it to a friend in his own words.

## The reader

- Young men, 17 to 22. Some grew up in church, some did not. Some read English as a second language.
- They read one day at a time, five to ten minutes, often on a phone or in bed.
- They are smart. They are not theologians, and they do not read self-help books.
- Write the way a trusted older man talks across a kitchen table: plain, calm, direct, warm. No hype, no therapy talk, no sermon voice.

## The one test for every sentence

Ask: could an 18-year-old explain this sentence to a friend right after reading it, and does he know what it asks him to see or do?

If not, rewrite it so it says plainly what it means. Usually that means:

- **A person doing something.** Put a person (you, he, God, a friend) as the subject and give him a normal verb: picks up, says, waits, skips, keeps, notices.
- **Name the thing.** Replace the abstract noun with what it looks like in real life.
- **Say the point, not a riddle about it.** A short punch line is fine only when it is instantly clear ("Weight has to go on it." "Begin there."). A short line that needs decoding ("Formation does not conclude. It consolidates.") must become a plain sentence ("You keep growing after today. What you built these forty days settles in and gets stronger.").

Plain does not mean longer. Cut words that do no work. A plain sentence may be a little longer than the riddle it replaces, but the whole section should not grow more than about 15 percent. Mix short and medium sentences. Most sentences: 6 to 16 words.

## What must not change

These are hard rules. Breaking one is worse than leaving a sentence abstract.

1. **Meaning.** Every claim keeps its meaning. You clarify, you never soften, sharpen or change a claim. Theology especially: say the same thing about God in plainer words.
2. **No new facts.** No new events, details, names, numbers, examples, feelings or backstory in the scenarios or anywhere else. If a plain version would need an example that is not in the text, keep the abstract sentence and list it under "questions for the author".
3. **Scripture.** Every line starting with ">" (epigraphs) stays exactly as it is, character for character. "Scripture says ... (Deuteronomy 6:16)" and "(1 Thessalonians 5:23)" keep their references and meaning.
4. **Headings.** Every heading line stays exactly as it is: "# ...", "## Day N: Title", "### The Scenario", "### What This Day Is Really About", "### The Practice". Two exceptions you may simplify if the words are abstract: the contrast-table title ("### X vs. Y", keep the "vs." form) and the words after "### Healing Forge:" (keep that prefix).
5. **"*End of Day N*"** lines stay exactly as they are.
6. **Names and numbers.** Every scenario name stays. Every number, year, verse reference and day range stays.
7. **Tables.** Same number of tables, same number of rows, exactly two cells per row, the separator line "| :- | :- |" unchanged. Each row keeps its left-versus-right meaning. Cells stay short (two to five words). You may simplify the words.
8. **The author's analogies stay**: the rope, the railing, the fever, the radio, the rower, the dashboard, the bicycle, the mirror, the flat tire, grammar, the swimming manual, the eight rowers, the long run, the blanket, the sentry, the current, the fire drill, the violinist, the house, the sailor, the builder testing a wall, steel and fire, water in the fuel line.
9. **Fixed sentences chosen by the author.** Leave these exactly as written:
   - Introduction: "You are not beyond repair."
   - Day 7: "God often steers you through your attention, and where your attention stays, your life follows."
   - Day 30: "Feeling distant is not the same as being abandoned." and "His silence is not neglect. He has not let go of you."
   - Day 38 table row: "| Advertises conviction | Carries it quietly |"
   - Appendix C: "You are not measured by momentum. You are formed by faithfulness." (the book's key line)
   - Appendix B must still say that "Healing Forge is part of" the training or formation, and is no detour from it (the author chose "part of", not "foundation"). You may simplify the rest of that sentence.
10. **Day 34, "Witness is not performance. It is presence that stays the same from one setting to the next."** The author named this exact sentence as too abstract. Rewrite it in plain words. Keep both halves of the meaning: being a witness is not putting on a show, and it means being the same man in every place (school, work, home, online). Keep it to at most one contrast.
11. **The author's voice.** First person "I" only in the Introduction. "You" everywhere else. Scenarios in past tense, third person. Short sentences in pairs are the author's rhythm; keep that feel, but never four very short sentences in a row.
12. **Format.** Keep the Markdown exactly: blank lines, list markers "- ", bold and italics where they are, straight quotes " and ' in body text (the epigraphs keep their curly quotes). No new headings, no new bold.

## Patterns that are blocked (the checker will fail you)

- No dash characters U+2014 or U+2015 anywhere. No spaced en dash " – " in sentences. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or a full stop.
- At most one two-sentence contrast per day ("X is not A. It is B.", "You are not A. You are B.", "This is not A. It is B."). Write the positive thing first. The checker counts these per day.
- No "not X, but Y". No "not because". No "This day is not about". No "This is different." No "The question is". No signposts ("Here is the thing", "Let me be clear", "One more thing", "Here is what").
- No new "For some men" and no new "quietly". Remove some where you can.
- No rhetorical question that you then answer. No triad of abstract nouns ("clarity, consistency, and courage").
- No AI words: journey, navigate, unlock, embrace, delve, testament, tapestry, profound, transformative, empower, foster, leverage (as a verb), robust, seamless, realm, landscape, resonate, elevate (as a verb; "ELEVATE" the phase name stays), holistic, nuanced, pivotal, vital, crucial, key (as an adjective). The full list is in the Ezra-edit skill (references/slop-blacklist.md).
