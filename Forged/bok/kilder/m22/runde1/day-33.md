## Day 33: Strength That Serves

> “Whoever would be great among you must be your servant.”
>
> Mark 10:43

Strength matures in silence. It is built on the inside as you use self-control, show up every day, and keep going. But it does not stay inside you. Sooner or later, it has to turn outward and serve others.

### The Scenario

Jordan had become the guy people could count on. His manager at the tire shop let him close alone, and he stayed calm when a customer started yelling. Then the requests came. The widow next door asked him to help clear her gutters. The youth pastor asked him to lead the ninth-grade guys. Jordan said no to both. Serving felt like a distraction from his own growth. By spring his strength served only him.

### Status vs. Service

| Status | Service |
| :- | :- |
| Draws attention | Uses strength for others |
| Wants credit | Accepts going unnoticed |
| Judges by reactions | Judges by faithfulness |
| Guards his strength | Puts it to work |
| Builds a brand | Builds trust |

Strength is given to carry weight for others.

### What This Day Is Really About

What matters is which way your strength faces. Titles and leadership roles have little to do with it. If your growth stays hidden, it stays unfinished, even if your discipline was real. Strength is mature when others can call on it.

Serving means offering your strength freely and asking nothing back. When His disciples argued about rank, Jesus said, "Whoever would be great among you must be your servant," and He lived it all the way to the cross.

### The Practice

Think about the people around you this week. Ask:

- Whose request for help have I been putting off?
- What am I good at that someone near me needs?

**Today's Action:** Before bed, help one person for ten minutes and tell no one. Take your neighbor's trash cans to the curb, or fix the loose cabinet door your mom mentioned last week. Pick something small enough to finish today.

### Healing Forge: When Serving Once Meant Being Used

Serving others can leave scars. You may have been relied on but never protected, needed but never seen. That taught you to pull back. The serving asked of you here is your own choice, and it has limits. Serving this way will not cost you who you are. It lets what has grown in you flow out to others.
