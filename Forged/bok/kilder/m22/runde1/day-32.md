## Day 32: The Discipline of Finishing

> “Let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up.”
>
> Galatians 6:9

Energy comes easily at the start. Finishing takes endurance. Talent will not get you there, and neither will going all out or the thrill of something new. Most men give up near the end, when progress feels ordinary and the reward seems far away.

### The Scenario

In September Ian promised to tutor a seventh grader from church in math every Tuesday until June. For the first month he made flash cards and looked forward to it. By February it was the same fractions at the same kitchen table, and nobody said thanks anymore. Nothing had gone wrong. He started thinking about meeting every other week, or taking a break until fall. It felt like wisdom. It was a way of never finishing.

### Quitting vs. Finishing

| Quitting | Finishing |
| :- | :- |
| Chases fresh excitement | Keeps his word |
| Calls losing interest wisdom | Admits he is tired |
| Wants a clean restart | Carries it to the end |
| Avoids the boring stretch | Sticks it out |
| Leaves things half-done | Turns effort into growth |

Finishing means getting the work done. It does not have to be perfect.

### What This Day Is Really About

Finish what you began. A house four-fifths built shelters no one. Leaving things half-done wears down your trust in yourself and in your own word, even when the work itself did not fail. What gave out was your endurance.

Finishing trains your will and your faith to stay with a task until you are free to put it down. Paul tells us not to "grow weary of doing good," and Jesus finished His Father's work on the cross.

### The Practice

Pick one small thing you started and let stall. Ask:

- What did I promise, and to whom?
- What does done look like, in one sentence?

**Today's Action:** Before bed, spend ten minutes on the next step that moves it closer to done. Send the email you owe, sand the next board for the shelf, or outline the last section of the paper. Then write down the date it will be finished.

### Healing Forge: When Finishing Once Meant Loss

Endings may have brought you pain: people leaving, fights, closeness taken away. Stopping early felt safer than risking disappointment at the end. Finishing what you started brings things together. Finishing is not abandonment. You are allowed to close what you no longer need to carry.
