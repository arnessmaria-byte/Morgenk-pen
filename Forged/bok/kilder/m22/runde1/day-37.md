## Day 37: Living With Eternity in View

> “We look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.”
>
> 2 Corinthians 4:18

How far ahead you look decides how patient you are. What you expect to last shapes what you are willing to go through. Most anxiety shrinks your view to today. Growing in faith widens it.

### The Scenario

Theo felt rushed even when nothing was due. Stuck behind a slow driver on the way to class, he tailgated, fuming. When a friend left him on read for an hour, he spent the hour guessing why. He refreshed his email twenty times a day, waiting to hear about a job. He still prayed and still went to church. But he lived as if everything had to be settled by tonight.

### Right Now vs. Eternity

| Right Now | Eternity |
| :- | :- |
| Wants it fixed today | Makes room to obey patiently |
| Counts today's cost | Weighs how it ends |
| Gets upset at delays | Can handle waiting |
| Turns up every feeling | Keeps your view steady |
| Shrinks what you see | Helps you hold out longer |

With eternity in view, urgent things still matter. They just take their proper place.

### What This Day Is Really About

Today is about seeing things at their real size. Thinking about eternity does not pull you out of your life. A sailor steering by the wave in front of him is seasick within the hour. Steering by the horizon, he is steady in the same sea. When you only look at right now, everything feels heavier than it is.

When you keep eternity in mind, you can handle a hassle without panic and pay a price without bitterness. You do not need to rush what was meant to take time. Paul looked "to the things that are unseen" because he knew the God who raised Jesus would raise him too.

### The Practice

Think of the last time you felt rushed or annoyed. Ask:

- What did I treat as urgent that could wait?
- What part of it will still matter in eternity?

**Today's Action:** Before bed, mute the app or chat you keep checking for an answer, and leave it muted until noon tomorrow. Then slowly read 2 Corinthians 4:16-18, and pray one sentence that hands the timing to God.

### Healing Forge: When Rushing Was How You Survived

Rushing can be a habit you learned to protect yourself from loss, punishment, or being left behind. Waiting felt dangerous. Looking toward eternity, you still carry your responsibilities. You just see them against a longer future. You are allowed to look further ahead.

*End of Day 37*
