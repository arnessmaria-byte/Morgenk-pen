## Day 26: The Morning You Don't Feel Like Facing

> “O LORD, in the morning you hear my voice.”
>
> Psalm 5:3

Some mornings fight you before the day starts. Your body is slow, your willpower is low, and your purpose feels far away. What you do then shows whether your training holds up with nothing carrying you.

### The Scenario

James's alarm went off at 6:15. No test was waiting, and his shift at the bike shop did not start until noon. He just felt heavy. He hit snooze twice, then lay on his back scrolling while the gym bag he had packed the night before sat by the door. His Bible stayed shut on the nightstand. He got up at 7:40, already behind, and spent the rest of the day catching up.

### Avoiding the Day vs. Showing Up

| Avoiding the Day | Showing Up |
| :- | :- |
| Hits snooze | Gets up at the alarm |
| Waits for motivation | Acts without it |
| Scrolls first | Prays first |
| Feels justified | Feels steady |
| Drags the whole day down | Sets the day's rhythm |

The day asks you to show up. Excitement is optional.

### What This Day Is Really About

You know the argument you have with yourself each morning under the blanket. Answer it with the first act of the day, before you feel ready.

Discipline follows a direction you have already chosen. It does not wait for your feelings to agree. Each morning tests that choice. In Christ you can pray David's words, "in the morning you hear my voice," and mean them on the heavy mornings too.

### The Practice

Look back at your last few mornings. Ask:

- What does my hand reach for when the alarm goes off?
- What do I tell myself to earn ten more minutes?
- Which part of my day pays for a slow start?

**Today's Action:** Before bed, set one alarm, not three. Tomorrow, put your feet on the floor at the first alarm. Before you look at any screen, say one sentence of prayer out loud. Do not try to make it perfect. Make it yours.

### Healing Forge: When Mornings Feel Heavy

Mornings may once have filled you with dread: conflict, demands, responsibility with no one to help you carry it. Avoiding them protected you. Healing invites you back gently, and you do not have to be perfect. Showing up can be quiet. What counts is that you choose it. If the heaviness will not let up, talk to a pastor, a doctor, or a licensed counselor.
