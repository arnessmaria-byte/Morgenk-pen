# Introduction

## What This Book Is For

This book was written to form you.

For over twenty years, I tested what would hold. I tested claims about what is true the way a builder tests a wall: I put weight on them. Some gave way. Others turned out to be scaffolding, useful for a while but never built to carry a roof. What was left was the foundation.

In 2016, I was called. In 2018, I committed myself. The testing has not stopped since. By 2022 only one path was left for me: Jesus, and the life that comes from Him. This book was forged on that path.

You are not beyond repair. But you are being shaped, whether you choose it or not, by habits, silence, fear, the easy way, and pain that has not healed. This book is here to interrupt the shaping you never chose.

## Where This Book Stands

This book stands on Scripture, the only rule that judges everything else and is judged by nothing: "Scripture cannot be broken" (John 10:35). It stands on the faith the church confessed at Constantinople in 381 (Appendix B): one God, the Father, the one source of all; one Lord Jesus Christ, "God from God, Light from Light, true God from true God, begotten, not made, of one essence with the Father"; and the Holy Spirit, who proceeds from the Father and is given to us through the Son (John 15:26; Galatians 4:6).

In God there is order without lesser worth. The Father sends, the Son is sent, and the Spirit proceeds from the Father, yet the three are one God, equal in glory. Paul ties the order of creation to that order: "the head of every man is Christ, the head of a wife is her husband, and the head of Christ is God" (1 Corinthians 11:3). Order does not mean lesser worth, whatever a demasculinized culture tells you and many churches now repeat.

Postmodernism tells you that no truth binds you. Radical feminism and woke ideology call every God-given difference oppression. That same culture wants young men passive and easy to steer, and a feminized, apostate church has traded the apostles' teaching for the approval of the age. Christ rebuked the church in Thyatira for tolerating "that woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess" (Revelation 2:20), and that spirit still reaches for the pulpit. Every reader is called to repent, and I am first in line: turn from the lies of the age and come back to the apostles' teaching.

## Why Another Devotional Isn't Enough

Most devotionals try to inspire you. They give you words for what you believe and few tools for handling pressure. Inspiration disappears the moment a hard Tuesday shows up.

Young men rarely fail because they think their faith is wrong. They fail because they have not trained it. Pressure does not ask what you believe. It shows what you have practiced.

This book assumes you are sincere and untrained. It cares whether your faith holds when no one is watching, when you do not feel like it, and when old habits come back.

## Forged, Not Polished

Compliments do nothing for steel. Fire, resistance, and repetition do the work. That is why this book is called *Forged*. Being shaped costs something, and integrity takes a long time to earn.

## Who This Book Is For

This book is for young men who:

- Believe, but feel untrained
- Are sincere, but not steady
- Want integrity more than emotional highs
- Sense they are being shaped by things they did not choose

It will not hype you up, flatter you, or fix you quickly. It cannot take the place of a church, mentors, or your own responsibility. If you want to be shaped, it was written for you.

## The FORGE Path

The forty days follow one training cycle, FORGE. Each letter names a phase.

**FLARE (Days 1–8): Ignition.** Faith that has been sitting idle gets shaken awake and starts to become your own.

**OBSERVE (Days 9–16): Awareness.** You start to see what has been shaping you without you noticing: screens, silence, peers, money, anger, relationships, private habits.

**REFINE (Days 17–24): Living it out.** What you believe moves into what you do. You stop facing things alone and start standing with brothers.

**GRIT (Days 25–32): Endurance.** Discipline becomes a steady rhythm. These days cover your mornings, tiredness, anxiety, taking care of your body, and finishing what you start.

**ELEVATE (Days 33–40): Turning outward.** What was built inside you now shows in how you lead, how you speak, and how you live your faith in front of others.

Each phase builds on the last. Walk the whole path.

## How This Book Works

Each day moves from a short opening and a scenario to a comparison, an explanation, one concrete practice, and a Healing Forge section. A few scenarios come from Scripture. Most are ordinary: failures no one sees, small compromises, the tension most young men carry but have no words for.

The practice is the point. You are not asked to feel anything, and the days you feel nothing count as much as the days everything feels clear. What you repeat becomes a reflex, and your reflexes decide who you are under pressure.

Read one day at a time. Each practice takes about ten minutes: two or three questions, then one Today's Action. You can do every Today's Action before 10 p.m. the same day.

If you miss a day, do not go back to Day 1. Starting over again and again is how men quit for good. Reread the last day you finished, go on to the next one, and count coming back as part of the practice.

Walk the forty days with someone if you can: a friend, a father, a mentor. Ask them to sharpen you, not to supervise you.

Every day ends with a Healing Forge section, because some things choke your growth the way water in a fuel line chokes an engine: pressing harder on the gas will not fix it. Anxiety, anger, hurt from relationships, sexual shame, feeling numb toward God, and old pain about your father all work this way. Each section names what is going on, points you to confession and Christ's cleansing where sin is involved, and gives you one solid step to take.

This book is not a counselor. If you carry deep trauma, harm done to you, grief that will not let up, or thoughts that frighten you, go to a pastor, a doctor, or a licensed counselor. Going is part of the training: the same honesty this book asks of you everywhere else, brought to the right place. If you might hurt yourself or someone else, get emergency help now.

## Before You Begin

None of the forty days ahead earns you anything from God. He accepted you before you began, and that was settled at a cross you did not carry. Training is how you answer that love. A son trains because he lives in his father's house, not to get adopted.

Begin there.

# Appendix A: The FORGE Cycle

*A plan that holds up in real life*

Training that stays in your head goes nowhere. Most young men who walk away from their faith were never argued out of it. When pressure came, their faith was still only an idea. The FORGE Cycle is here to turn that idea into practice.

FORGE is a training rhythm you repeat. Through it, your faith moves from words you were taught to the way you live every day. Each phase is a stage of growth you need. Skip a stage and you become fragile. Rush one and you burn out. Keep coming back to them, and you will last.

The order matters. Repetition alone will not change a man who is divided inside. That is why you observe and heal before you work on endurance, so you do not build habits on top of a crack that has not healed.

If you practice the habits but skip the healing, you end up better organized on the outside and still not whole inside. None of this means you make yourself holy. Scripture says that God Himself makes you holy (1 Thessalonians 5:23). The cycle describes your part in His work and never replaces it.

**FLARE: Identify What Ignites You.** Flare is the moment something starts to bother you: irritation, discontent, longing, or a belief that will not leave you alone. It feels rougher than inspiration. Flare asks one question: *What pressure keeps coming back in my life?* Ignore it, and your faith stays borrowed from others. Name it, and your faith starts to become yours. Flare shows you why your faith has to be real to you and not just look respectable.

**OBSERVE: Test Your Faith in Real Life.** In Observe, you stop guessing and start watching. Instead of promising to change, you notice how your faith holds up under stress: screens, peers, tiredness, conflict, time alone. It trains you to be honest without condemning yourself, and it swaps vague guilt for knowing exactly what is going on. You cannot refine what you refuse to see.

**REFINE: Let Truth Shape Who You Are.** Refine takes what is true and makes it part of how you live. Scripture used to give you insight. Now it becomes a mirror. It judges everything else and is judged by nothing (John 10:35; Acts 17:11). Test what you hear as the Bereans did, "examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so" (Acts 17:11). We read Scripture from the Hebrew Masoretic text and the received Greek text. Your choices start to follow God's word instead of your impulses. Refine hurts because it shows where you are split. It also pulls your life together, so your faith stays the same from place to place.

**GRIT: Practice Under Pressure.** Grit makes your growth stable through repetition. It builds a daily rhythm for the days when motivation runs low. You choose discipline ahead of time to protect yourself, not to punish yourself. Grit trains you to keep showing up through mornings, tiredness, desire, and silence, and to finish what you start. This is where your faith proves it can last. Loyalty carries it more than feelings do.

**ELEVATE: Live Your Faith in the Open.** In Elevate, what was built inside you comes out into the open. Your faith starts to shape how you lead, speak, see things, influence others, and carry yourself. People can count on your faith, and it is not for show. Your maturity shows when you stay steady, control yourself, and serve, without announcing it.

# Appendix B: Healing Forge

*Why healing is part of the training*

Discipline alone cannot heal the parts of you that pain has shaped. Many young men try to outrun open wounds by keeping routines, pushing themselves, or trying harder to be good. When that fails, they decide they are weak. Often the real problem is an injury, and more discipline will not heal it.

Some of what weighs on you is a wound: grief, hurt from relationships, long-lasting anxiety. It can leave you on guard, numb, or angry, and that is often mistaken for spiritual failure. Some of it is sin. Healing Forge is not therapy, and no technique can wash a conscience. Sin is confessed to God and washed away in Christ's blood: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9).

Every day ends with a Healing Forge section, because many of these pressures started long before you came to faith. Having them does not mean your faith is missing. Each section asks the same of you. Name the wound without drama. Confess the sin plainly, to God and, where it helps, to a trusted brother (James 5:16). Then take one steady step.

Healing begins when you stop hiding the wounds and the sins that still shape you. Nobody is asking you to blame your parents or to share it all in public. You are asked to tell the truth to the right people, and to keep telling it.

A clean conscience and a clear head make discipline something you can keep up. That is why Healing Forge is part of the training and no detour from it.

These pages can help you name a wound. They cannot treat it. Where the wound is deep, take it to a pastor or a licensed counselor. Going takes courage.

## The Creed of Constantinople (381)

This is the faith the church confessed in 381, given here without the later Western addition "and the Son."

We believe in one God, the Father Almighty, maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible.

And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all ages, God from God, Light from Light, true God from true God, begotten, not made, of one essence with the Father, through whom all things were made. For us and for our salvation he came down from heaven, was made flesh by the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary, and became man. He was crucified for us under Pontius Pilate, suffered, and was buried. On the third day he rose again according to the Scriptures. He ascended into heaven and is seated at the right hand of the Father. He will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead, and his kingdom will have no end.

And in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and giver of life, who proceeds from the Father, who with the Father and the Son is worshiped and glorified, who spoke through the prophets.

In one holy, catholic, and apostolic Church. We confess one baptism for the forgiveness of sins. We look for the resurrection of the dead and the life of the age to come. Amen.

# Appendix C: How to Continue After Day 40

When the book's daily plan ends, you will drift unless a new routine takes its place. These forty days were scaffolding. What you built in them now has to become your own. These steps help you keep going without starting the book over.

**1. Revisit One Phase Each Month.** Growth comes in cycles. Choose one FORGE phase each month and go back to its practices on purpose. For example:

- Month 1, OBSERVE: track how you react under pressure
- Month 2, GRIT: strengthen one daily discipline
- Month 3, REFINE: take a fresh look at what you believe and what you do

Return to it. You do not need to start over.

**2. Set Three Non-Negotiables.** Choose three realistic practices that steady your life under pressure. For example:

- One short pause with God, every day
- One boundary that keeps your head clear
- One weekly check-in with a trusted person

If everything is a priority, nothing is.

**3. Replace Motivation with Structure.** Do not wait until your feelings line up with your beliefs. Schedule what matters, set up your surroundings, and make fewer decisions on the fly. Routines last longer than inspiration.

**4. Say Your Faith Out Loud.** Every few months, say in your own words what you believe and why. Faith you never speak dries up. Faith you say out loud gets clearer.

**5. Expect Resistance.** Resistance means you are still being shaped, so do not take it as a sign to stop. When progress feels slow, keep doing what is right.

**6. Do Not Continue Alone: Stay Planted in a Church.** This book gave you practices to do on your own, and that was never the whole plan. You grow up in your faith inside a church. There you hear preaching that can correct you. You stand beside older men who have carried what you are learning to carry. And at the Lord's table, you receive grace without earning it. A book cannot know your name. A church can. If you have no church home, make finding one your first assignment after Day 40. It does not need to be perfect.

Do not sit under a pulpit that bows to the spirit of Jezebel. Do not fund or lend your name to a congregation that has women pastors (1 Timothy 2:12-13), blesses what God calls sin (Romans 1:26-27; 1 Corinthians 6:9-11), or punishes men who hold to Scripture. Women and men bear God's image equally (Genesis 1:27). The office Christ gave is a different question. And no church may bless what Christ died to wash away. Paul reminded the Corinthians that some of them had lived that way, and they were washed. That washing is offered to every reader who repents. Find tested men who stand on the apostles' teaching, and build brotherhood with them (2 Timothy 2:2).

## A Final Reminder

You are not measured by momentum. You are formed by faithfulness. You do not need to do more than keep living what you believe.

That is how the work you started here keeps going.
